# Measurement methodology and limitations

This document defines exactly what the system measures, how, and what it cannot measure.
It describes the implementation in `backend/glovesize/` (reference) and `frontend/src/vision/`
(browser port).

## 1. Capture

The customer holds an open hand, palm towards the camera, with a US quarter lying flat on the
centre of the palm. The browser processes the live video. For each frame:

1. **MediaPipe Hand Landmarker** (pretrained, `hand_landmarker.task`, float16 v1) returns 21
   image landmarks (pixels) and 21 *world* landmarks. World landmarks are the model's metric
   3D estimate around the hand centre. They are used only for plausibility gates and are
   never reported as measurements.
2. A **coin search window** is placed around the palm centre (mean of landmarks 0, 5, 9, 13, 17).
   Its size is `0.75 × palm length`, and the coin diameter is expected to be between `0.12` and
   `0.7 × palm length`.
3. **Coin detection** (§2) runs on that window only.
4. **Live checks** give immediate guidance. Frames that pass every check are collected,
   at most one every 60 ms, until 24 frames are collected.
5. Only numbers are sent to `/api/scan`: landmarks, the coin ellipse with its quality metrics,
   and brightness. **No pixels leave the device.** The server re-applies all gates
   authoritatively.

## 2. Coin detection

The reference implementation is `coin_detection.py`. The browser port is `coinDetector.ts`; a
parity test checks it against fixtures generated by the Python reference.

| Step | Method |
|---|---|
| Pre-processing | Grayscale → CLAHE (clip 2.0, 8×8 tiles) → 5×5 Gaussian blur |
| Candidate generation (compared) | (a) **Chroma segmentation**: Otsu threshold of the normalised warm-chroma channel `(R−B)/(R+G+B)`. (b) **Contours**: Canny → closing → convex-hull circularity ≥ 0.72 → `fitEllipse`. (c) **Hough**: `HoughCircles` (HOUGH_GRADIENT). Duplicates are merged and the method names recorded. |
| Refinement | For each candidate: 144 rays from the centre. Along each ray, the strongest outward *rise* of the smoothed chroma channel is found with sub-pixel parabolic interpolation, and an ellipse is fitted to those points with MAD outlier rejection. A second, narrower (±8%) pass follows. |
| Quality metrics | Edge support (fraction of rays with a consistent edge), largest angular gap, RMS fit residual, edge width (contrast ÷ peak gradient, a blur proxy), glare fraction (pixels ≥ 250 inside the coin), coin/skin contrast |
| Acceptance | Hard gates on every metric (see `config/measurement.json → coin_detection.accept`), plus a heuristic quality score ≥ 0.6. The score is the geometric mean of linear ramps. **It is a detection-quality heuristic, not a probability.** |

**Why the chroma channel.** On synthetic scenes where the quarter's raised rim is brighter than
the skin, grayscale edge detection locks onto the rim's inner edge. That measures about 74 px
for an 80 px coin, a 7.5% scale error. Cupronickel coins are achromatic and skin of every tone
is warm (R > B), so the normalised chroma channel has its strongest step exactly at the coin's
outer boundary and is largely insensitive to shading. The consequence is that **only
silver-coloured references are supported**: a copper penny would not work. In colour mode,
the synthetic tests recover diameters to within about 0.3 px (+0.3%), which is the
anti-aliasing bias of the synthetic renderer.

**Rejected conditions** (all tested on synthetic scenes): partial occlusion (as little as 10%
coverage of the edge), strong glare, heavy blur, coins below 28 px, no coin present.

## 3. Calibration: from the coin to the hand

`pixels_per_mm_at_coin = coin_major_axis_px / 24.26`

This establishes scale **only at the coin** (top face, palm centre). Extending it to the
landmarks uses three explicit steps, each with an uncertainty term.

### 3.1 Tilt (perspective of a tilted palm)

A circle viewed at tilt τ appears as an ellipse with `minor/major = cos τ`. The major axis is
not foreshortened (weak perspective), which is why scale uses the major axis.

* τ > 20° → frame rejected (`coin_tilted`).
* 8° ≤ τ ≤ 20° → landmarks are **affinely rectified**: they are stretched by `major/minor`
  along the minor-axis direction, about the coin centre. This assumes the palm is planar and
  the coin lies flat on it.
* τ < 8° → no correction. Noise biases small-tilt estimates and the correction would be under
  1%. The residual `(1 − cos τ)/√3` is added to the uncertainty.

### 3.2 Depth layers (the coin and the landmarks are at different depths)

The coin's silhouette is its top face, about 1.75 mm above the palm skin. MediaPipe landmarks
are joint centres, roughly 10 mm (prior ± 5 mm) below the palmar skin. Points farther from
the camera look smaller, so:

`px_per_mm_at_landmarks = px_per_mm_at_coin × Z / (Z + Δ)`, with `Δ = coin thickness + landmark layer depth`.

`Z` (camera-to-coin distance) is estimated from the coin's pixel size, assuming a horizontal
field of view between 55° and 80° (browsers do not expose camera intrinsics). At typical
distances (250–400 mm) the correction is about 3–5%, and it is **not negligible**: without it
the measurements would be biased low. The correction can be switched off
(`depth_model.enabled`) so that the validation study can A/B test it.

### 3.3 Remaining perspective, non-planarity and lens distortion

Fingertips and wrist are not exactly in the coin's plane: hands are not flat, and a tilted
palm puts the two ends at different depths. Lens distortion grows towards the image edges.
These are covered by a 1% relative term (`perspective_rel_sigma`) and by gates:

* the hand must stay within the frame margins;
* the hand must occupy at least 30% of the shorter image side;
* 2D finger bend must be ≤ 25°;
* MediaPipe's 3D estimates must show finger bend ≤ 35° and palm tilt ≤ 30°.

These terms are **not** physically measured. A single RGB view cannot recover them, which is
why the validation study (see `VALIDATION_PLAN.md`) must quantify the remaining error.

## 4. Measurement definitions

All lengths are in millimetres, in the rectified palm plane, as the median over accepted
frames after MAD outlier rejection.

| Key | Definition | Note |
|---|---|---|
| `hand_length_mm` | Straight line from the wrist landmark (0) to the middle fingertip (12) | Approximates the glove-industry definition "distal wrist crease to tip of middle finger". Landmark 0 is not the crease, and the offset must be calibrated. |
| `palm_length_mm` | Wrist (0) to middle-finger MCP joint centre (9) | |
| `mcp_span_mm` | Index MCP (5) to little-finger MCP (17) joint centres | **Narrower** than anthropometric palm breadth (skin edge to skin edge) |
| `index/middle/ring/little_length_mm` | Joint-chain length MCP→PIP→DIP→tip | Starts at the joint centre, which is proximal to the palmar finger crease used in anthropometry |
| `thumb_length_mm` | Thumb MCP (2)→IP (3)→tip (4) | The thumb rotates out of the palm plane, so it is never better than "low confidence" |
| `palm_breadth_mm` | Skin edge to skin edge at the metacarpal heads | **Not measured** unless entered by the customer, or estimated by a linear model fitted on reference data |
| `hand_circumference_mm` | Around the knuckles, excluding the thumb | **Not measurable from a single view.** Customer tape entry (±4 mm assumed), or a validated model only. |
| Proportions | Ratios of raw landmark distances | Independent of the coin scale |

Fingers that are bent in a frame are excluded from their own measurement. A bent middle finger
rejects the frame, because hand length depends on it. Values outside plausible human ranges
are rejected. An implausible hand length usually means the wrong coin was selected.

## 5. Uncertainty model

Each measurement reports a 1σ uncertainty and a 95% interval. Before calibration the
components are combined in quadrature:

| Component | Source |
|---|---|
| Frame variation | Standard deviation across frames ÷ √n (random) |
| Landmark localisation | `1.5 px` at the model's 224 px input, scaled to the hand's image size (√2 for a distance). Systematic, not reduced by averaging. |
| Coin scale | `hypot(0.5 px edge-definition floor, coin tolerance) / coin diameter` |
| Tilt | Correction uncertainty (4° tilt-estimate σ) or the uncorrected foreshortening residual |
| Depth layer | Uncertainty of Z (FOV range) and of the landmark-layer depth (±5 mm) |
| Perspective & flatness | 1% of the value |
| Anatomical definition prior | Unknown offset between landmarks and anthropometric points (hand length: 5 mm). **A prior, not a measurement.** |

After calibration (`measurement_calibration.json` with `status: calibrated`), the uncertainty
becomes `hypot(validated residual SD, frame variation)`. The UI then reports the basis as
"validated".

Status: **valid** if the 95% interval is within ±8% of the value, **low confidence** if within
±14%, otherwise **invalid** (the value is hidden).

**Consequence for sizing today.** With the priors above, hand-length σ is about 6–7 mm. Many
glove charts use size steps of about 10–12 mm, so the uncalibrated system usually reports
"uncertain" or "borderline" and asks for a tape-measure confirmation. This is intentional. The
recommendation becomes decisive only once a validation study shows the real error is smaller.

## 6. Sizing

`glovesize/sizing.py` applies the manufacturer's chart (`config/sizing_charts/*.json`) as rules:

* Each size has `[min, max)` ranges per measurement. A value exactly on a boundary goes to the
  larger size; a value in a gap is assigned to the larger size.
* The `primary_only` rule uses the first measurement. The `largest_size` rule computes a size
  per measurement and takes the largest.
* Levels:
  * `clear`: ±2σ lies inside one size.
  * `likely`: ±1σ lies inside one size; an alternative is given.
  * `borderline`: ±1σ spans two sizes; both are named.
  * `uncertain`: ±2σ spans three or more sizes; the customer is asked to confirm by tape.
* A missing required measurement (e.g. circumference) returns `needs_more_data`, with
  instructions for measuring it.

These levels describe **measurement uncertainty only**. They are not probabilities that a glove
fits, and no percentage is displayed.

## 7. Known limitations

1. A single RGB view cannot measure circumference or palm thickness, or reliably measure
   anything out of the palm plane. Circumference requires a tape or a model validated on
   reference data.
2. Landmarks are joint centres predicted by a neural network. Their offsets from skin creases
   and fingertip edges differ between people. Calibration removes the mean offset; the
   person-to-person spread remains in the residual.
3. The depth correction relies on an assumed FOV range and an assumed landmark-layer depth.
4. The 3D flatness gates use MediaPipe's *estimated* world landmarks. On a relaxed sample hand
   photo they gave a 34° palm tilt and 25–35° finger bends, while 2D bends were under 6°. The
   thresholds (30°/35°) are provisional and may over-reject real users. Tune them on pilot data
   by watching the rejection rate.
5. Only silver-coloured references are supported. Coin identity cannot be verified from the
   image, so the customer or operator confirms it, and an implausible implied hand size is
   rejected.
6. Lens distortion is not corrected (no intrinsics in the browser). It is mitigated by keeping
   the hand away from the frame edges.
7. Synthetic-image tests show the algorithms recover known geometry. They are **not** evidence
   of real-world accuracy.
